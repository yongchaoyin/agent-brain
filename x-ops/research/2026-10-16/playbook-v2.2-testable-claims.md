# Testable claims from playbook.md (v2.2)

## @Gotwei 打法 (style)
An English AI-news account that curates and creates: fast "COMPANY 🔥: / BREAKING:" news posts, leaks and previews (testingcatalog/btibor91 style), quote and interview clips, comparison cards, infographics, and first-person hands-on tests. Each post adds a "Why it matters" take, carries a screenshot or short video, and puts the source in a self-reply. Growth is driven by replies: early, substantive replies under big AI accounts. Benchmarks: testingcatalog, btibor91, kimmonismus, ai_for_success, AiBreakfast, rowancheung, minchoi.

## Claims (C# : claim — what confirms / what refutes in a creator's top posts, frequency, and replies)
1. Posts with media beat plain text on impressions (66% of accounts, 1.28×). Confirm: most top posts by relative impressions carry media. Refute: plain text makes up a share of top posts equal to or larger than its base rate.
2. Video beats plain text (67%, 1.50×) and is the strongest format. Confirm: video is over-represented among top posts (≥3× median). Refute: no video in the top posts even though the account posts video.
3. Images give only a small gain (58%, 1.15×). Confirm: image posts sit only slightly above the median. Refute: image posts dominate the top posts (a strong effect) or fall below text.
4. Quote posts underperform (0.81×). Confirm: quotes are rare among top posts and below the median. Refute: quotes show up often among top posts.
5. Long posts (>280 chars) get more impressions (1.27×) and much higher bookmark rates (74%). Confirm: long posts are over-represented among top posts by impressions and bookmarks per 1k. Refute: top posts are mostly short.
6. Short one-liners (<120 chars) are the most likely to go viral (16%) but have low engagement rates. Confirm: many top posts are one-liners plus video. Refute: no short posts among the top posts.
7. External links in the post body don't hurt (73%, 1.12×; the v1 "link penalty" was withdrawn). Confirm: top posts include ones with links in the body. Refute: posts with links are consistently in the bottom group.
8. "BREAKING / NEW / COMPANY 🔥:" openers lift posts (3.22×, but only n=4). Confirm: top posts open this way more often than the account's other posts. Refute: they do no better than ordinary openers.
9. Emoji give no gain (0.87×, withdrawn). Confirm: emoji use is the same in top posts and the rest. Refute: emoji posts are clearly over-represented at the top.
10. First-person writing has almost no effect on impressions (1.03×) but slightly higher engagement. Confirm: neutral on impressions. Refute: first-person posts dominate the top posts.
11. Leaks, exclusives, and hands-on or benchmark content beat ordinary launch reposts. Confirm: top posts are scoops or own tests. Refute: top posts are plain relays of official announcements.
12. Screenshot + one-line context is the highest-volume original format. Confirm: top posts are mostly screenshots with one line. Refute: other formats lead.
13. Infographics, cheatsheets, and lists get the most bookmarks. Confirm: list or infographic posts have the highest bookmarks per 1k. Refute: their bookmark rate is at or below the median.
14. Posting time (Beijing 21:00–02:00) barely matters (0.98×). Confirm: no time-of-day pattern in top posts. Refute: top posts cluster in particular hours.
15. Posting frequency doesn't decide results (successful accounts post anywhere from <1/day to 50/day). Confirm: posts_per_day is uncorrelated with median_impressions or follower growth. Refute: a clear correlation.
16. Most posts from top accounts are replies (50–90%). Confirm: reply_pct is mostly ≥0.5. Refute: reply_pct is mostly low.
17. Replying to big accounts (≥100k followers) earns about 2.6× the relative impressions of replying to accounts under 100k. Confirm: the creator's highest-impression replies are under big accounts. Refute: their best replies are under small or mid accounts.
18. Reply length doesn't move impressions, except that substantive 200+ char replies do better (0.083 vs 0.036–0.039) and likes rise with length. Confirm: long, substantive replies get the most impressions and likes. Refute: short agreement replies do just as well.
19. Self-replies get about 3.6× the impressions of replies to others and are mostly used to add content (only 12% carry links). Confirm: self-reply impressions are high and mostly add detail or a source. Refute: self-replies get low impressions.
20. Timing: top replies land within 1–3h of the parent post, and within 30 min on mega-posts. Confirm: high-impression replies come early. Refute: late replies also get impressions.
21. Generic praise, snark, and "thanks" replies get about 0 engagement; pushback or a concrete added fact gets traction. Confirm: top replies contain facts or counterpoints. Refute: one-word replies do well (except from established big accounts).
22. In the 0–1k follower stage, growth comes mainly from replies, and accounts stick to one fixed format. Confirm: small accounts that grew had high reply_pct and a consistent format. Refute: they grew mainly from originals or changed formats often.
23. English beats Chinese for audience size (Chinese AI-news accounts get 2–3K impressions with the same format). Confirm: Chinese-language peers have lower impressions per follower. Refute: they are comparable.
